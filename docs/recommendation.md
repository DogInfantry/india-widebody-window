# Recommendation

## The answer

**Compete with the Gulf hubs. Do not fly more aircraft to them.**

The Gulf corridor is the prize and it always was: 36.9M passengers, four times
India's entire direct Europe market, and roughly 8.1M of those passengers a year
are not going to the Gulf at all. They are going to Europe and North America, and
paying a Gulf carrier for the privilege. That contested traffic is worth between
**INR 27,500 and 53,900 crore**, a third to two thirds of IndiGo's annual revenue.

But the instrument is not more Gulf flying. It is **long-haul direct service from
Indian gateways that makes the Gulf connection unnecessary**, and the wide-body
order is exactly the equipment that buys it.

Sequence: **Europe first, North America second, Gulf capacity held roughly flat.**

---

## Why this is a refinement of "Gulf first", and what moved it

This project's headline was "reclaim the Gulf corridor first, long-haul second".
Three lines of evidence, none of which existed when that was written, now say the
aircraft cannot go to Gulf airports even if the traffic is there.

**1. There is no room to fly them.** India-Dubai already runs at **89.6%** of its
reported bilateral entitlement. The headroom is about **13,800 one-way seats a
week**, roughly 0.72M seats a year, against a corridor carrying 36.9M passengers.
Adding wide-body capacity to the single largest India-Gulf city pair is not a
commercial decision, it is a treaty negotiation India has so far refused.

**2. The unit economics are the worst on the map.** Scale IndiGo's published unit
cost across the corridors and the Gulf has the **least** room of any of them to
absorb a yield decline, because sectors that short keep cost per seat kilometre
high. On these numbers Gulf sectors do not cover their own cost at IndiGo's
realised yield. Europe could absorb about a fifth off the fare and still clear.

**3. The order book is sized for a longer network than this one.** The 140 firm
wide-bodies carry 119.3bn ASK, which would add **78%** to what Indian carriers fly
internationally today. Holding share needs about half that. The book clears only
if the average sector lengthens roughly **27%**, to 4,345 km, or if Indian
carriers take **58%** of the market. Pointing those aircraft at 2,200 km Gulf
sectors leaves most of the capacity doing nothing the existing narrow-bodies could
not do.

**The reconciliation.** The Gulf traffic splits into roughly **28.9M genuinely
point-to-point** passengers, the diaspora and labour corridor that Kochi and
Kozhikode feed, and **8.1M connecting** passengers whose journey continues west.
The first group is real, defensible and already served by narrow-bodies that are
adequate for it. The second group is the prize, and it is won by flying past the
Gulf, not to it.

So the corridor stays the strategic centre of the case. The aircraft point
somewhere else. Recorded as pivot 6 in [the pivot log](pivot_log.md).

## The options, and what each needs to be true

Time to capacity and capital intensity are judgements and are written as words,
not scored on an invented scale. The two columns that are computed are marked.

| Option | Time to capacity | Capital | What would have to be true |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Own wide-bodies on long-haul** (recommended) | 2027 at the earliest | High | Deliveries arrive on a schedule no primary source states, and Europe direct yields hold within about a fifth of today's realisation |
| Own wide-bodies on Gulf routes | 2027 at the earliest | High | A bilateral renegotiation India has refused for a decade, **and** unit economics that are currently negative turn positive |
| Lease or damp-lease bridge | Immediate | Low, recurring | Lease rates leave headroom over the corridor's own cost. **Not quantifiable here**: transaction rates are paywalled trade press and no assumption row can clear |
| A321XLR on thin routes | Near term | Medium | The corridor sits inside **8,700 km**, which covers Europe and excludes North America *(computed)*, and demand is thin enough to suit a narrow-body |
| Codeshare or joint venture | Immediate | None | A partner hub is worth more than the margin ceded, which reverses the recapture logic the rest of this case rests on |
| Do nothing | n/a | None | The connect gap stays with the Gulf carriers and the order book already placed is deployed at today's sector length |

The recommendation is the first row because it is the only option that is
simultaneously available (unlike a Gulf bilateral), affordable in unit cost terms
(unlike Gulf sectors), reachable (unlike North America by narrow-body), and
capable of absorbing capacity already contracted for.

**The bridge question is left open, and deliberately.** Whether to damp-lease
wide-bodies before 2027 turns entirely on lease rates, and those are paywalled.
This project does not have the number and will not model one to fill the gap. It
is named as the largest single unresolved input in the recommendation.

## Roadmap

**Phase 1, now to first delivery: prove the demand without the metal.**
Europe direct is already 8.8M passengers. Establish which of those routes support
wide-body economics before the aircraft arrive, using the narrow-bodies and
partners already flying. Hold Gulf capacity flat: there is no entitlement to grow
it with and no unit-cost case for doing so.

**Phase 2, first delivery onward: Europe direct, from more than one gateway.**
Every corridor inside 8,700 km is narrow-body reachable, so the wide-body's job is
density and cabin mix on the routes that are already thick, not opening thin ones.
Delhi's largest European route is London at 1.40M passengers, then Istanbul,
Paris, Frankfurt and Amsterdam.

**Phase 3, once Europe recapture is demonstrated: North America.**
It has the most cost headroom of any corridor and cannot be reached by anything
except a wide-body. It is also the smallest at 2.5M direct passengers, the most
exposed to the airspace closures that lengthen the sector, and the place where
777X delivery risk sits. It is a second move on the evidence, not a first.

**Purchase rights are the option, not the plan.** The 40 unconverted rights should
stay unconverted until Europe recapture is measured. Converting them commits
capital against a network shape that has not yet been demonstrated.

## What would have to be true

Stated so the recommendation can be falsified rather than defended.

- **Direct yields hold within about a fifth of today's realisation on Europe.**
  That is the computed headroom. Below it, Europe direct stops covering its cost
  and the whole sequencing argument fails.
- **The average sector actually lengthens.** If the wide-bodies end up flying Gulf
  and Southeast Asia rotations, the order book is roughly twice what the network
  needs and the capital is stranded.
- **Bilateral entitlements do not expand sharply.** Counter-intuitively, an
  India-UAE expansion would *weaken* this recommendation, by letting Gulf carriers
  re-flood the corridor faster than Indian carriers can build direct networks.
- **Deliveries slip less than about two years.** Beyond that the bridge question
  stops being optional, and the bridge cannot be priced with public data.
- **The connect traffic is genuinely recapturable.** The 8.1M figure rests on an
  origin-destination share that cannot be verified. If the true split is closer to
  DGCA's sector reading, the prize is smaller and the case thins.

## Risk register

Likelihood and impact are judgements, so they are words. What matters more, and is
the reason this is a table rather than a scatter plot, is the last two columns:
what would falsify the risk, and what to watch.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | What would falsify it | Leading indicator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Delivery slip** | High | High | A published Airbus delivery schedule for the IndiGo book, which does not currently exist | Airbus quarterly deliveries; any IndiGo fleet-plan disclosure |
| **Rupee depreciation** | High | High | A sustained period of USD/INR stability | FBIL daily reference rate. Currency added +0.41 to FY2026 CASK, more than the entire net rise |
| **Gulf carriers cut fares on direct-competing routes** | Medium | High | Gulf capacity discipline as their own fleets tighten | Published fares on India-Europe versus India-Gulf-Europe itineraries |
| **Bilateral expansion re-floods the Gulf corridor** | Medium | Medium | India holding its refusal, as it has since 2014 | Ministry of Civil Aviation announcements; Rajya Sabha questions on air services agreements |
| **ATF spike** | Medium | Medium | Brent stability | IOCL monthly ATF price, international and domestic bases separately |
| **Europe direct yields fall past the headroom** | Medium | High | Yields holding within a fifth of today's realisation | IndiGo quarterly yield disclosure, which is published |
| **Crew and MRO cannot absorb wide-body induction** | Medium | Medium | Type-rated pilot numbers, which nobody publishes | DGCA licence issuance; announced MRO capacity |
| **Airspace closure lengthens sectors** | Medium | Medium | Reopening of Pakistan or Russian overflight | Route-level great circle against filed flight time |
| **The connect prize is smaller than modelled** | Medium | High | A published origin-destination table, which IATA sells rather than publishes | Any free O-D release; a second agency covering India-Gulf |

**The single most likely reason this recommendation fails** is the last one, and
it is a data risk rather than a market one. Only **5.6%** of India's international
traffic is cross-checked against a second agency. The Gulf, which carries half of
it, has no equivalent open source at all, so the checks that caught five earlier
errors in this project cannot be run on the corridor the case turns on.

## Out of scope, and why

- **Financing.** Sale-leaseback against JOLCO against operating lease changes who
  owns the aircraft, not where it should fly. It is a real question and the
  lease-rate data that would settle it is paywalled.
- **Airport slots at Indian gateways.** Material at Delhi and Mumbai, but a
  capacity question rather than a corridor-choice one.
- **Belly cargo.** Freight moves through the pipeline already and is unused.
  Second-order for this decision, real money on wide-bodies, and named in
  `ROADMAP.md` as an extension rather than quietly dropped.

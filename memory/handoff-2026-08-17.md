## Open decisions the next session must make

1. **Utilisation definition: owned fleet or active fleet?** DGCA `aircraft_hours` gives IndiGo
   FY2026 1,614,608 hours / 441 aircraft = 10.03 hr/day, against a reported ~13. Cross-checks
   are clean (2.05 hr/departure, 534 km/h block speed), so the gap is grounded aircraft from the
   Pratt & Whitney engine problem. DGCA therefore measures **owned-fleet** utilisation. A
   capacity question arguably wants **active-fleet**. This is a judgement call, not a data
   problem, and it must be documented on the chart either way.
2. **A350-900 seat count if the planning PDF resists.** Airbus publicly describes the A350 as
   leading the "300-410 seat category". A low-300s three-class layout is defensible **only if
   the configuration is stated on the chart**. IndiGo has not published its config.
3. **Profit pool segment split.** The total margin anchor is real and primary-sourced (EBITDAR
   26.3% FY2025, 27.3% FY2026 ex-forex). The split across domestic narrow-body, international
   point-to-point, connect traffic and ground is modelling work with no public source. It must
   carry the `MODELED` badge on the chart face.

## Things I got wrong this session, so they are not repeated

Recorded because each was caught by something, and knowing *what* caught it matters.

1. **Published a wrong margin finding.** Claimed IndiGo's operating margin halved 22.3% to
   14.0% and called it the squeeze funding wide-bodies. It came from an aggregator's
   "operating profit" line that IndiGo does not publish. Real EBITDAR margin *improved*. Caught
   by the user's verification pass, retracted in `docs/methodology.md`. The lesson is in the
   `UNVERIFIED_NO_PRIMARY` status: a plausible number that can never be checked is more
   dangerous than a missing one.
2. **Offered a fake cross-validation.** Claimed an "88 to 89% ancillary split" corroborated the
   transcribed yield and RASK. RASK 4.51 was actually Q4 FY25 **CASK**, so I was comparing a
   yield against a cost. It proved nothing.
3. **Declared IOCL unreadable** on the strength of one failed fetch method. Firecrawl read it
   fine. One failed method is not an unreadable page.
4. **Dropped UK CAA on a timebox without a real attempt**, leaving India's largest European
   market (3.5M pax) with no cross-check. Going back to it found the corrupt 2019 UK figure.
5. **Used `--ours` during a rebase**, which means *upstream*, and silently discarded the anomaly
   correction from the committed parquet. Caught only because I checked the file contents
   afterwards instead of trusting the rebase.
6. **Invented a job requirement.** The gap analyzer's phrase-drift check flagged a "Quality
   Assurance" row whose phrase appears nowhere in `jd.txt`. Deleted rather than reworded.
7. **Hallucinated context-window percentages** for several turns, escalating past a stale hook
   reading with no new signal. Do not report context percentages without an actual reading.

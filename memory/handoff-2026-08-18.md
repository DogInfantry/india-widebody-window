## The thing that actually happened: the answer changed

The headline was **"reclaim the Gulf corridor first, long-haul second"**. It is now
**"compete with the Gulf hubs, do not fly more aircraft to them"**, Europe first.

This was not a stylistic rewrite. It fell out of three findings that did not exist before:

- **No room.** India-Dubai runs at **89.6%** of its reported bilateral entitlement, about
  0.72M seats a year of headroom against a 36.9M corridor.
- **Worst unit economics on the map.** Gulf sectors have the least yield headroom of any
  corridor (**-4.3%**) and do not cover their own cost at IndiGo's realised yield.
- **An order book sized for a longer network.** 119.3bn ASK, **+78%** on today's international
  capacity, **1.94x** the growth needed to hold share. Clears only at sector +26.8% or share 58.2%.

The reconciliation: of 36.9M Gulf passengers, ~28.9M are genuinely point-to-point (Kerala
diaspora, already well served by narrow-bodies) and ~8.1M are connecting onward, worth
**INR 27,486 to 53,906 crore**. That second group is won by flying *past* the Gulf.

**The hypothesis tree had already reached this and nobody propagated it.** Branch 4.3 said
incremental Gulf capacity "has to go long-haul, or to Gulf points with slack, or wait on a
renegotiation India has so far refused". It sat there and never reached the headline. Branch
5.4 now asks whether the aircraft can be *deployed*, not just whether the demand exists, and
that question is what inverted the answer.
